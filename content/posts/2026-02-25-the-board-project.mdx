When Noah and I walked into the shaping bay to make it official, one of the guys was halfway through fixing a busted board. He didn't stop for us. He just talked us through the repair as he went, and Noah stood next to me and watched the whole thing in complete silence.

I took that as a good sign.

The deal itself happened on the phone before that. I called the crew at [Santosha](https://santoshasurf.com/), we talked about how Noah actually surfs, and only then did I pitch the project (the backstory is in [my last post](/blog/objects-born-not-made)). By the end we had a rough structure and an agreement that we'd get our hands a lot dirtier than we'd first planned.

The board is ordered. The raw foam blank takes about eight weeks to reach the island, which is a long wait but a useful one: we get to prepare before we touch a single tool. Once it lands, a few weeks of shaping, painting and glassing. Twelve weeks, start to finish.

Noah is 8, so this has to feel like an adventure, not a school curriculum. I also want us to take the craft seriously enough that we walk away having learned something real. We aren't there to spectate.

I want this to be something we do together, not one more chore wedged between school and dinner. So every week, we commit to three things:

## Week 3: Design Theory

<div className="flex flex-wrap gap-3 my-4">
  <span className="text-xs px-3 py-1 rounded-full bg-blue-100 dark:bg-blue-900/30 text-blue-700 dark:text-blue-400 font-semibold">~3 hours</span>
  <span className="text-xs px-3 py-1 rounded-full bg-gray-100 dark:bg-gray-800 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400">🎯 Understand why water cares about curves</span>

Nothing on a surfboard is there to look cool (the paint job aside). Every curve does something to the water. A concave bottom channels it differently from a flat one. A sharp rail lets go of it faster than a soft, rounded one. This week is the physics of our board specifically.

  <span className="text-xs px-3 py-1 rounded-full bg-purple-100 dark:bg-purple-900/30 text-purple-700 dark:text-purple-400 font-semibold">~4 hours</span>
  <span className="text-xs px-3 py-1 rounded-full bg-gray-100 dark:bg-gray-800 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400">🎯 Build muscle memory on low-stakes surfaces</span>
</div>

The artwork goes on with Posca pens: water-based, non-toxic, punchy as hell, and forgiving in the hands of an eight-year-old. Paint behaves differently on raw foam than on paper, though, so we run drills on scraps before we go anywhere near the real board.

<div className="my-4 space-y-2 pl-1">
  <label className="flex gap-3 items-start"><input type="checkbox" disabled className="mt-1.5 w-4 h-4 shrink-0" /><span>Stock the arsenal: a fresh set of [broad-tip Posca pens](https://greenlightsurfsupply.com/products/posca-surfboard-paint-pen-broad-tip).</span></label>
  <label className="flex gap-3 items-start"><input type="checkbox" disabled className="mt-1.5 w-4 h-4 shrink-0" /><span>Ask Daniel about surface prep: how hard do we sand, and do we wipe it down with acetone first?</span></label>
  <label className="flex gap-3 items-start"><input type="checkbox" disabled className="mt-1.5 w-4 h-4 shrink-0" /><span>Scavenge the bay for offcuts, scrap foam, or busted blanks to use as our canvas.</span></label>
  <label className="flex gap-3 items-start"><input type="checkbox" disabled className="mt-1.5 w-4 h-4 shrink-0" /><span>Run paint drills to get used to the friction of the foam and practice blending colors.</span></label>
  <label className="flex gap-3 items-start"><input type="checkbox" disabled className="mt-1.5 w-4 h-4 shrink-0" /><span>Read up on [Drew Brophy's legendary Posca techniques](https://drewbrophy.com/why-im-giving-away-my-surfboard-painting-process-using-posca-paint-pens/) to avoid rookie mistakes.</span></label>
  <label className="flex gap-3 items-start"><input type="checkbox" disabled className="mt-1.5 w-4 h-4 shrink-0" /><span>Journal: log the tests, especially the disasters, so we don't repeat them.</span></label>
</div>

<div className="my-6 p-5 rounded-xl bg-amber-50 dark:bg-amber-900/10 border border-amber-200 dark:border-amber-800 text-sm">
  <strong>Checkpoint:</strong> Can Noah tell a clean Posca line from a messy, bleeding one at a glance?
</div>

<div className="my-6 p-5 rounded-xl bg-amber-50 dark:bg-amber-900/10 border border-amber-200 dark:border-amber-800 text-sm">
  <strong>Note to self:</strong> Posca dries fast but stays water-soluble. Skip a proper seal of clear acrylic and the fiberglass resin will smear the artwork into a muddy blur.
</div>

## Week 10: Sanding + Refinement

<div className="flex flex-wrap gap-3 my-4">
  <span className="text-xs px-3 py-1 rounded-full bg-orange-100 dark:bg-orange-900/30 text-orange-700 dark:text-orange-400 font-semibold">~5 hours</span>
  <span className="text-xs px-3 py-1 rounded-full bg-gray-100 dark:bg-gray-800 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-400">🎯 Trusting our hands to find the perfect symmetry</span>
</div>

Most of what gets romanticised as "shaping" is sanding. Slow, careful, exhausting sanding. It's the part that turns a crude chunk of foam into something that slides through water, and this week we obsess over symmetry and foil.

<div className="my-4 space-y-2 pl-1">
  <label className="flex gap-3 items-start"><input type="checkbox" disabled className="mt-1.5 w-4 h-4 shrink-0" /><span>Attack the rough shape with the surform and sanding screens to establish the core profile.</span></label>
  <label className="flex gap-3 items-start"><input type="checkbox" disabled className="mt-1.5 w-4 h-4 shrink-0" /><span>Work up the grits: start at 80, graduate to 120, and finesse it with 220 until it's buttery smooth.</span></label>
  <label className="flex gap-3 items-start"><input type="checkbox" disabled className="mt-1.5 w-4 h-4 shrink-0" /><span>Trust our hands: eyes closed, palms down both rails at once, feeling for bumps. Then double-check with calipers.</span></label>
  <label className="flex gap-3 items-start"><input type="checkbox" disabled className="mt-1.5 w-4 h-4 shrink-0" /><span>Earn the final nod of approval from Daniel before we're allowed to touch the paint.</span></label>
  <label className="flex gap-3 items-start"><input type="checkbox" disabled className="mt-1.5 w-4 h-4 shrink-0" /><span>Journal: get macro shots of the foam texture changing as we move up through the sandpaper grits.</span></label>
