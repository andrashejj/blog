Mauritius has made this difficult. We have One Eye here, which, for me, is one of the best waves in the world. We can surf, kitesurf, work and build things while living close to the ocean. It's really hard to get anything better than this.

And still, I've started planning the ultimate year of life and work.

It's fucking unreal that we even get to think about this. To sit here and discuss which island we might live on, where we'd surf, how we'd work and how we'd bring up our children. We have the power to make decisions that change what an ordinary day of our life looks like. I don't want to get so used to that freedom that I stop noticing it.

I can't think of another time when a normal person could look at a map like this and seriously plan to take their work and family along. Keep earning, keep building things, stay connected to people on another continent, and make a home somewhere for a few months. For someone doing the kind of work I do, the range of choices feels almost absurd.

And I want to use it. I want us to have a say in where our time goes, what our children grow up around and how much of the day we spend outside. Some of these ideas will turn out to be impractical. I'd still rather work through them and find out which ones we can make happen than dismiss the whole thing before we've tried.

The exact locations aren't set in stone. I keep coming back to Southeast Asia and Fiji, places where we could stay for months, get to know the water and have enough quiet to get on with our projects. Somewhere remote enough to feel close to nature, with a connection good enough to keep working.

I want to surf in the morning, kite when the wind comes up, and have proper stretches of time to build stuff. I'd like a bit more room in the water, too. Fewer people competing for the same wave. Time to be with ourselves without always having somewhere else to go.

We'd also be taking our family life with us. Homeschooling, a daily routine with a baby, finding help, having people visit. All of that needs to work in the same week as the surfing and the projects.

The first version has three stops pencilled in, with South Africa, Australia, New Zealand and Hawaii still in the mix.

## May to August: Rote or West Sumbawa

Rote Island is high on the list, especially around Nemberala and T-Land. West Sumbawa is the other possibility, with Scar Reef and Yo-Yo's among the waves I'd want to look into.

The attraction is getting farther out into Indonesia, beyond Bali, and staying long enough for a place to become familiar. [Rote's dry-season surf window](https://rotesurfhouse.com/surfing-in-rote/seasons/) fits this part of the year. Whether the nearby kite spots, accommodation and work setup fit us still needs checking. A good surf break doesn't automatically make a good place to launch a kite.

The version I have in my head is a bungalow near the water, boards close by and a desk where I can get something done. I'd happily make the extra journey for that.

Some of the early research was a little too generous. Two to four people in the water, fast Starlink in every bungalow, four months on a visa on arrival. Those are nice things to put in a plan. They're also things to check before buying tickets. Indonesia's [visa on arrival covers 30 days, with one further 30-day extension](https://jogja.imigrasi.go.id/how-to-get-an-indonesia-visa-on-arrival/). A four-month working stay needs its own visa research.

And I'd want to test the internet at the actual house. A speed number in an accommodation listing won't tell me whether a call holds up when everyone gets home from surfing.

## September to December: Fiji

Fiji is the place I keep picturing. Reef passes, early boat rides and a few months living around the ocean.

The Coral Coast looks like a possible base. Frigates is one of the waves that caught my attention, a long outer-reef left reached by boat. [Tourism Fiji puts the trip from the Coral Coast at roughly 30 to 45 minutes](https://www.fiji.travel/things-to-do/surfing/fijis-top-surf-breaks). I'd need to understand what that means for an ordinary Tuesday: who takes us out, what it costs and when conditions make the trip possible.

The Lau Islands are also on the list, though that feels closer to an expedition than a base for a regular working week. I'm drawn to the remoteness. I haven't worked out the practical side yet.

September through December is a pencilled-in block. It crosses a change in seasons, with [Fiji's cyclone season beginning in November](https://www.fiji.travel/things-to-know/healthy-and-safety/natural-disasters). Surf, wind and boat access need to be considered together before choosing where to stay and for how long.

The work hours might be the bigger problem. Starting European calls after dinner sounds fine until I put the end of the working day next to an early boat departure.

## January to April: maybe Peru

Pacasmayo made the shortlist because of the long left. The thought of riding a wave with a kite for minutes at a time is enough to make me consider a very different setting from the tropical islands.

It's a point break rather than a coral reef, with a cooler-water, desert-coast setting. I can see the appeal. But I'm less attached to this stop than to Indonesia and Fiji, and the dates need work.

[Wild Kite Peru describes its wind season, including Pacasmayo, as mid-April to mid-January](https://wild-kitesurf-peru.com/faq/). That leaves most of my proposed block outside it. Peru might belong somewhere else in the year, or this part of the route might become somewhere else entirely.

I also don't think any of these places can promise fewer than five people in the water. That's what I'd love to find, but I won't build a year around a guaranteed empty lineup.

## Other places still in the mix

South Africa could fit the early part of the year. Around Cape Town, [the summer wind blows hardest in January and February](https://www.capetown.travel/survival-tips-for-cape-town-summer/). It also makes the working day much easier: [South Africa is on UTC+2](https://www.timeanddate.com/worldclock/south-africa/cape-town), so European hours would leave me sleeping at night. Finding a quieter base with the right waves would be the next question.

For Australia, I'd look more closely at the west coast. Margaret River is a possibility for surfing; Geraldton and the surrounding coast are worth investigating for kiting, with [a wind-sport season running roughly October to April](https://www.australiascoralcoast.com/destinations/geraldton-surrounds/geraldton). They're different bases, so I'd need to choose which stretch of coast suits the months and the kind of riding we want to do.

New Zealand belongs on the list too. [Taranaki's Surf Highway 45](https://www.taranaki.co.nz/visit/highlights-of-taranaki/surf-highway-45) gives me somewhere specific to start looking: surf breaks around the coast, wave sailing at Kina Road and kiting at East End Beach. I'd want to understand the local wind patterns before choosing dates. It would also bring back the problem of fitting European calls around sleep.

And Hawaii, probably Maui as a starting point. The timing needs care there: [winter brings the north-shore surf](https://www.gohawaii.com/islands/maui/things-to-do/water-activities/Surfing), while [a local kite school puts the most reliable wind in summer](https://mauikiteboardinglessons.com/about/kiteboarding-faqs/). I'd need to check where those conditions overlap, what a longer stay costs and how much room we'd find in the water.

Any of these could replace a stop or change the order. I want a few places where we can settle in for a while, so adding another possibility means being willing to drop something else.

## Put the working day on the calendar

The time difference needs more attention than I gave it in the first version. Using a Central European 09:00–17:00 working day, with the seasonal clock change included, the local hours would look roughly like this:

| Proposed stay | Local hours for a full European working day |
| --- | --- |
| Rote or West Sumbawa, May–August | 15:00–23:00 |
| Fiji, September–December | 19:00–03:00, then 20:00–04:00 after Europe changes clocks |
| Peru, January–April | 03:00–11:00, then 02:00–10:00 after Europe changes clocks |

Those use the current offsets for [central Indonesia](https://www.timeanddate.com/time/zones/wita), [Fiji](https://www.timeanddate.com/time/zone/fiji/suva) and [Peru](https://www.timeanddate.com/time/zone/peru/lima). They're a useful check on how much of the European day I could realistically overlap with.

Fiji looks very different with a 4 AM finish written next to it. For this to work, I'd need to agree on a smaller window for calls and do more of the building in my own daytime. Otherwise the morning surf session and the working day would be competing with sleep.

## The family week still needs working out

Homeschooling is one of the bigger things to sort. We need to decide what we're teaching, what support or materials we'd use, and who sits down to do it each day. Exploring a new place gives us plenty to learn about. We still need time for the schoolwork that takes practice and a bit of patience, including on a morning when the waves are good. And we need to think about friendships and time with other kids when we're staying somewhere for months.

With a baby, the day also has to make room for feeds, naps, getting out of the house and bedtime. An early boat trip means one of us is away for several hours. A late work call means someone else is handling whatever happens at home. We need to work out those handovers, leave each other some time, and have enough slack for a day after a rough night.

Right now, Abigail helps us three days a week, eight hours each day. That's 24 hours of help already built into our life in Mauritius. When I picture us working and getting into the water somewhere else, I need to account for that support. We'd have to work out what help we'd need, what we could afford and how we'd find someone we trust in each place. Moving every few months would mean doing that again.

Work needs some hours we can rely on. Schoolwork needs attention. We'd also want to explore beyond the nearest surf break, go somewhere together and have days where we can stay out longer. I can easily fill a proposed day with things I want to do. The harder job is putting names next to who's doing what, who's with the children, and when each of us gets a break.

Then there are people visiting. We'd love to have family and friends spend part of the year with us. That affects which house we rent, how much room we need and when we choose to stay put. We'd want time to enjoy their visit, so those dates need to sit alongside work commitments and the children's routine. A spare bed alone doesn't sort that out.

And we should plan visits to Switzerland and Hungary too. Hungary is home, maybe. Mauritius is where we're living now. We want time to see people in Switzerland and Hungary, catch up and spend a few ordinary days together. Those neat four-month blocks already fill the year, so we'd need to shorten some stays to make room for these visits and the travel between them. I'd like to put that time on the calendar while the route is still open.

I'm still most drawn to Indonesia and Fiji. Before committing to a house, I want to sketch an ordinary week there with school time, help, work, baby care and time in the water all on the same calendar. Then try a week with visitors, or a week when the help falls through.

Just dare, just go, just be you.
