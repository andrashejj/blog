Starting a surf club is a questionable way to reduce your workload. Adding a granola project suggests you didn't understand the assignment.

And yet [Sunset Duckies](https://sunsetduckies.com) gives me energy. Surfing, making things, getting people together. These take time and make me want to do more with the time I have.

I want to work really hard, move several projects forward and get a lot done. I also want to listen properly, be empathetic and stay calm when something breaks. The bug already exists; adding my mood creates a second problem with worse documentation.

## I'm done waiting for Saturday

The arrangement where you spend five days working and two days being “free” drains me. By Friday you've put a ridiculous amount of expectation on Saturday. Now relax. Enjoy yourself. Recover an entire personality before Monday.

Then the weekend arrives, you slump, and somehow you're still tired. You waited for it, and it gave you very little.

That rhythm doesn't suit me. I want things I care about throughout the week: work worth doing, people worth seeing, something physical, something unfamiliar. A surf session on a weekday can give me more than another evening spent waiting for the right day to enjoy myself.

A high-RPM life sounds exhausting from the outside. From inside, it can feel like the rush of a wave, growing fitness, curiosity, the youthful urge to try something before you've worked out whether you're any good. I recognise myself in that. Adrenaline is part of the thrill; sleep, food and recovery are what let me keep showing up.

People who love a firm Friday boundary should keep it. I want more choice about when I work, when I play and when I stop.

## One person, several things worth doing

I picture private life above and business below, with connections running through both. Surfing can leave me ready to tackle a difficult build. A conversation can become an idea. Finishing good work can give me confidence and the means to do something with friends. Duckies sits happily across the line, making a mess of the filing system.

Balance, for me, means making room for all of it. I can be fully with a person, then fully in a piece of work. Everything belongs to the same life; everything doesn't need to happen at once. The phone can stay on shore.

Research offers pieces of an explanation. Research on self-determination connects motivation and wellbeing with choice, competence and belonging. Recovery research includes learning and mastering something alongside relaxation and mentally switching off from work. A challenging hobby can fit into recovery. These are useful ways to understand Duckies, rather than proof that adding another commitment always helps.

There's evidence for spending effort and feeling more energetic, too. In a small trial of 36 otherwise healthy, sedentary young adults with persistent fatigue, six weeks of low- or moderate-intensity exercise improved feelings of energy. Low intensity had the better fatigue result. A punishing workout wasn't necessary.

Physical and mental health belong together here. A review of 218 trials found that activities including walking, yoga and strength training reduced depressive symptoms, although confidence in much of the evidence was low. Exercise can belong alongside established care. Nobody needs to be told to jog their way out of a mental illness.

For me, the return is practical. More willingness to start, more patience to finish, more interest in somebody else's problem. I can work intensely and still be approachable. When someone asks for a minute, I'd like to give them my attention rather than a live demonstration of how busy I am.

## ADHD gets a seat at the table

If curiosity, enthusiasm and unusual connections are strengths you recognise in yourself, value them. Build a life with interesting work, movement and variety. You don't need to become somebody else before you can do excellent work.

The research is more specific than the “ADHD is a superpower” slogan. A review of 31 studies found stronger evidence for divergent thinking in people with elevated ADHD traits below the diagnostic threshold than in clinically diagnosed groups. It also found no overall negative effect of stimulant medication on creativity.

ADHD can cause serious difficulties, and effective treatment and support matter. Embracing yourself includes getting help with the parts that hurt. You don't owe anyone a hidden genius to compensate for finding everyday things difficult.

I like having several projects to care about. Each still needs a turn. Write the new idea down, finish the current thought, let the granola wait until you've put the surfboard down.

## Consciously turning the volume down

I also want time to meditate, breathe and stop trying to solve things. Out in the water, I want to feel the board, watch the next set and be there. Being at ease with myself and part of nature is reason enough. The ocean doesn't need a productivity metric.

That quiet needs a place in an active day. Ten minutes without a screen. A walk without a podcast. Gentle breathing without turning it into another personal best. I want to be able to enjoy intensity and choose stillness.

Mindfulness programmes have some evidence behind them: a review of 47 trials found moderate evidence for small improvements in anxiety and depression. That supports making room for practice, without promising that meditation will empty your brain or fix everything.

Wim Hof belongs in this conversation as a separate, more intense practice. A 2024 systematic review found a small evidence base, with mixed exercise-performance results and substantial study limitations. I wouldn't treat it as a shortcut to unlimited energy. Its breathing exercises can cause fainting: practise seated or lying down somewhere safe, never in or near water or while driving. Surfing and breath-holding exercises stay separate.

And sleep gets its hours. In a controlled study, restricting time in bed to four or six hours a night produced accumulating cognitive deficits that people's sense of sleepiness didn't fully reflect. Feeling wired isn't proof you're rested.

Tomorrow can have a difficult build, a conversation worth taking slowly and time in the water. Tonight I can write down the next idea and leave it there.
