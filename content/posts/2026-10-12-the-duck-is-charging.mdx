I have ADHD. My brain seems to need a certain RPM to work well. Enough stimulation, enough going on, a bit of what feels like adrenaline. When I get that right, I can work exceptionally well. Doing less often makes things harder for me.

I like having several things going on. Surfing, building software, running [Sunset Duckies](https://sunsetduckies.com), starting a granola project. They take time, but I enjoy them and often feel better for doing them.

That pace can be hard on people I love. I want to work, build things and say yes to opportunities. I also want to listen and be there for someone without my mind wandering halfway through a conversation. I find that really difficult.

## I don't want to wait for the weekend

Working through the week and waiting for Saturday to enjoy myself doesn't suit me. I can put so much expectation on the weekend, then get there and feel tired and flat anyway.

I'd rather have the things I enjoy throughout the week. A surf before work, seeing friends, trying something new. Getting out in the water can leave me feeling ready to work again. I like the rush of a wave, getting fitter and having an idea I want to try.

Work and the rest of my life overlap quite a bit. Duckies is a surf club, time with people, and now a granola project too. I like that. A conversation can give me an idea to work on, and making progress with work can give me the energy and money to do other things.

I want to keep doing these things. I also need to leave time for people whose idea of a good week might be very different from mine.

## Being there for people

Saying yes feels good. Another project, a surf session, something to make with other people. It gives my brain something to do. Being told to slow down can be frustrating when being busy is helping me feel and work better.

I like my curiosity and enthusiasm. I don't want to treat everything about the way my mind works as a fault. But I can get caught up in what I want to do and be difficult to live with. The people I love need my time and attention, and I don't always give them enough of either.

I can care deeply about someone and still struggle to be present. That's painful. ADHD helps explain why I find it hard, but it doesn't make it any easier for the person trying to talk to me.

Sometimes I need to do less, even when I'd happily take on something else. I find it easier to get excited about another project than to stop and listen. I'm trying to get better at noticing that before it hurts someone.

## Slowing down

I've been trying breathing, meditation and making a conscious effort to listen. I want to hear what someone is saying without already thinking about something else. I don't find it easy, and deciding to pay attention doesn't always mean I manage it.

Being outside helps me practise that too. When I'm surfing, I want to feel the board, watch the next set and enjoy being there.

With something more intense like Wim Hof breathing, I keep it separate from surfing. [Those exercises can cause fainting](https://www.wimhofmethod.com/faq), so they belong seated or lying down somewhere safe, never in or near water or while driving.

I also need enough sleep and recovery. I can feel wired and want to do more when I need to rest.

I'm still working this out. I like my pace, and I want to be easier to be with. Before saying yes to the next thing, I need to look at what I've already committed to and whether someone is waiting for time I've promised them.
