## Overall design concept

A warm cottage interior leaning Japanese-Scandinavian, as long as we can stop it turning into beige theatre. Natural imperfections are good. Fake rustic is not.

### Guiding principles

- Comfortable, airy rooms, with indoors and outdoors running into each other.
- Natural materials you want to touch, in a warm neutral palette with sage and terracotta accents.
- Built-in furniture wherever possible, tough washable fabrics, and the tile-stove bench stitching the zones together.

### Key materials & textures

- Reclaimed and natural wood (oak, pine) for floors, beams and furniture; linen and heavy cotton in undyed tones.
- Lime plaster or clay walls; rattan and woven cane for panels and headboards.
- Natural stone and ceramic for counters and tile-stove details; matte sage and terracotta zellige in the bathrooms.

### Colour palette

- Base: cream, warm white, sand, natural plaster; woods in honey oak, warm pine, reclaimed timber.
- Accents: sage green and olive (Bathroom A, guest bedroom); terracotta and burnt sienna (Bathroom B).
- Metals: aged brass, matte black, brushed copper.

## Entry

- A bench wraps the tile stove, with built-in shelves above and baskets underneath. Maybe a second fireplace element on this side.
- Across from it: wood or wrought-iron coat hooks, open shelves, low shoe storage, and a built-in cupboard for coats and seasonal gear.
- The bench keeps going into the kitchen and living area, which ties the whole ground floor together.

## Wellness

- A sauna, hot tub and outdoor shower on the right side of the garden (as you face the house).
- Wellness retreat for the adults, wet play zone for the kids.
- Guiding principle: the easier it is to do, the better.

### Sauna

- A small, simple wooden sauna hut on a deck (light cedar with string lights and a glass door, or a dark-stained modern cabin).
- Towel hooks and a wicker basket outside.

### Hot tub

- A wood-fired barrel or round wooden soaking tub, ideally with a small firepit nearby.
- Adirondack chairs, lanterns, and string lights overhead.
- Doubles as a water feature for the kids.

### Outdoor shower

- A simple outdoor shower attached to the sauna or close by.
- A black wood panel wall with a copper rain shower on a gravel/pebble base.
- For rinsing off after the sauna or hot tub.

A wooden deck ties the sauna, hot tub and shower into one area. Open question: is wooden decking sustainable enough in the long run, or do we need an alternative?

## The palette

Scroll back up and the same threads run through every room: cream, sand, honey wood, sage, terracotta, reclaimed wood, linen, lime plaster, rattan, natural stone. Built-in furniture where it solves a real problem. Indirect lighting, never the cold ceiling glare of a rental flat. And nothing in the house you'd be scared to set a wine glass down on.
