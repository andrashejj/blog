We set out to build a surfboard. That was the whole plan.

The duck came later. Then the logo versions. Then the stickers. Then other kids wanted the same sticker on their boards. Then there was a club, with a name, before anyone had actually decided it was a club.

So here's the map, before the pieces scatter into half-stories:

- [The board](/blog/sunset-duckies-board): the three-month build at Santosha, from blank foam to finished 5'6" shortboard.
- [The duck](/blog/sunset-duckies-brand): how Noah's pencil sketch became board artwork through versions, votes, and arguments about what looked right.
- [The stickers and shirts](/blog/sunset-duckies-merch): what happened when the drawing left the board and started turning up on other kids.
- [The club](/blog/sunset-duckies-club): a few kids at Tamarin with the same sticker and no adult structure around it.
- [The first heat](/blog/sunset-duckies-competition): the board's first real test in the water.

## How it grew

The order matters.

If we'd started by announcing a small surf brand for a group of kids, I'd like to think someone would have stopped us. That's not what happened. We started with a board. The board needed artwork. The artwork went through the kids. The artwork made good stickers. The stickers went onto other boards. The other boards made the kids look like a crew. The crew needed a day in the water.

At every step, the next thing was only slightly ridiculous. That's how most projects get away from you.
