The danger with cleaning up a child's drawing is that you can fix it to death.

Noah drew the duck in pencil, quickly, at the kitchen table. It had the confidence of a thing that didn't know it would later be inspected by adults with software.

That makes it sound like a solo job. It wasn't. Noah gave us the duck and the attitude. Then came the versions: we showed them to the other Duckies, asked for votes, changed colours, changed type, brought back details we'd removed, and slowly found the one everyone recognised as theirs. My job was to make the duck printable, maskable and sprayable while keeping it theirs. The thing to avoid was turning Noah's sketch into my design.

<ImageFloat src="/images/board-project/IMG_4982.jpeg" alt="Noah's original pencil sketch: two board views labelled Back side, Logo, Pad, Lightning, with the note Sunset Ducky designed by Noah" side="right" width="44%">

The sketch was more of a spec than it looked. A circle near the nose with an arrow pointing at it: "Logo." A grid at the tail: "Pad." A bolt: "Lightning." Along the rail: "Sunset Ducky, designed by Noah."

The work was translation first, then negotiation.

</ImageFloat>

## The mascot

The duck works because it shouldn't. It's a bath toy in sunglasses and a backwards cap, riding a wave on a shortboard. Described out loud, that's a bad sticker from a tourist shop. In Noah's drawing it had just enough arrogance to get away with it.

Behind it sits a sunset, banded orange to yellow in a tight circle. That's half the name. The other half is the duck, because children are often better at naming things than adults. They stop before they ruin it.

The exact duck changed a lot. Some versions were too polished. Some were too babyish. Some looked like clip art. My opinion of any of them on a screen was close to useless. The test that worked was whether the kids still cared when they saw it.

## The wordmark, the tagline, the line

Under the logo, the name is set in a heavy condensed sans, all caps, stacked. It isn't subtle, and it shouldn't be. This is going on a child's surfboard, not above the door of a quiet hotel.

Below that, in a loose handwritten script: "main character energy." I would never have written that line, which is exactly why it stayed. It belongs to the internet Noah and the other kids are growing up inside, not to me.

The tagline lives on the bottom of the board:

"No cap" means no lie. On a board with a duck wearing a cap, it also picks up the kind of stupid second meaning you only get if you don't overthink it. Noah liked that. So did I.

## The palette

Three colours do most of the work: warm cream, teal, and the orange-yellow sunset. We tried others. We lined options up side by side and asked the kids, who were blunt in the way only children can be. Any version that needed explaining died on the spot.

The same pattern held the whole way through. Every time I polished, it got worse. Every time we went back toward the first drawing, or toward something the kids had reacted to instantly, it got better.

## From one board to everything

Once the artwork existed, it started turning up everywhere. A traction pad. Stickers. A few tees and caps. There was no strategy. Give a group of kids a duck logo they helped choose, plus access to printing, and the duck won't stay put.

<ImageFloat src="/images/board-project/IMG_5644.jpeg" alt="Noah applying the big Sunset Duckies sticker to the finished board at home, fins stacked beside him" side="left" width="44%">

My part was mostly restraint dressed up as design. Make it printable. Make it maskable. Make it clean enough to survive on foam under gloss. But don't make it sensible, and don't overrule the weird little preferences that made the kids point at one version and not another. Sensible would have killed it.

</ImageFloat>

The flames from the original plan never happened. That's probably the best design decision we made, and it wasn't mine. The finished duck isn't purely Noah's either. He started it, and the others pulled it into shape by caring enough to vote. It lives at [sunsetduckies.com](https://sunsetduckies.com) now, which still feels slightly ridiculous. Good.
