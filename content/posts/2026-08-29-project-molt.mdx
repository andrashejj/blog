Estelle has started her internship with Sunset Duckies. That gave us the reason to stop treating the brand as an idea sitting in my notes. Project Molt is now a proper piece of work for her, with a brief, deadlines, templates, deliverables, and decisions we can review together. That is much better than asking her to “help with the brand.”

The Project Molt brief currently has four product sheets. One says healthy granola, one says first surfboard, and the other two still say “not yet ideated.” That is deliberate, although it makes the granola look suspiciously like a decision already made.

So we wrote down a rule: no favourite and no maker conversations until all four ideas are equally clear. By 7 September, each needs a customer, a problem, a use around surfing, a quality promise, materials or ingredients, and a provisional price. Then we put the same four ideas in front of families, kids, buyers outside the club, and possible makers.

Sunset Duckies already has the easy parts of a small brand. It has a good duck, a group of kids who care about it, parents who help, and a real place behind the story. That also creates the easiest way to get this wrong. We could print the logo on an ordinary product, sell it to friendly parents, and mistake loyalty for demand.

The club cannot become a captive customer list. If a product only sells because a parent wants to support the kids, the product failed. A surfer should choose it because it is useful, well made, and worth the price. The local work and community model should make a good product more meaningful, not make excuses for a mediocre one.

In September we will choose one of the four using the interviews, likely costs, production limits, and what people reject as much as what they like. The selected idea becomes a small proof of concept. We will promote it before launching it alongside a surf competition in Tamarin at the end of October, with a visible price and a record of sales, orders, refusals, and product problems.

Then the slower test begins. From November through February we need a second product cycle, real economics, and a handover of useful decisions and assets to people in Tamarin. We also need to test the model with surf communities outside Mauritius. Tamarin is the first proof, not a mould that gets shipped around the world.

For now, the brief has four boxes and two of them are embarrassingly empty. That is the work until 7 September.
