Estelle is the daughter of a good friend of mine from Switzerland. She is travelling to Mauritius to do her internship with Sunset Duckies. I did not want to give her a vague assignment to “help with the brand,” so we made the brand exercise her project. We call it Project Molt.

By 7 September she will have rough directions for all four product categories: everyday wear, surf wear, surf gear and surf nutrition. We have basically decided to start with granola, so that is where the detailed work goes. The concept needs to say who it is for, when people would eat it, what goes into it, how it is packed, what it costs and why someone would buy it again.

After that we interview the families and kids, at least five people outside the club, and possible granola makers or suppliers. They see the granola concept and its price. We ask whether they would buy it, what would stop them, and whether another category solves a stronger problem. By 25 September we decide whether to make the first batch, revise the concept or change direction.

Sunset Duckies has a good logo and a lot of goodwill around the club. Parents could buy something to be nice and give us completely useless evidence. I want the product to sell to people who do not know us. It has to be useful, well made, and worth the price without the club story.

I am also not sure the club and product business should be the same thing. The club gives the product a good origin story, but that may get us a first sale rather than a viable business. We need to test the product without asking people to fund a good cause. For now I would keep the money separate. Granola revenue pays for making and selling granola. It does not buy surfboards. A later contribution to the club should be a decision we can afford and report.

We then make a small batch or sample and launch it with a surf competition in Tamarin at the end of October. Promotion starts before the event, with registration, the product, and the price made public as soon as they are ready. At the launch we record what sold, what did not, and why.

## Can 100 people carry it?

A group of 100 club supporters who buy once can fund a batch. A group of 100 repeat customers who pay full price and bring the next customer can support a small business. We need to learn which group we have.

We will record where each customer came from, whether they paid full price, whether they returned, and whom they referred. Then we calculate the margin after ingredients, packaging, production and delivery. For this project, word of mouth means one customer brings another without us approaching both. WhatsApp activity stays separate from sales.

I see four stages:

1. **0 → 1.** Someone outside the founding families buys the product at full price because they want the product.

2. **1 → 10.** The first buyers expose the obvious problems. Some return and some bring another buyer.

3. **10 → 100.** Production works reliably, repeat buying is visible, the margin can pay for the next batch, and sales no longer depend on one person asking everyone.

4. **100 → another community.** Someone outside Tamarin wants to gather the first local buyers and run the same test. They use the shared product and brand rules without copying everything about Tamarin.

At 100 we should be able to judge whether Sunset Duckies has a Tamarin product, a small sustainable business, or the first working community of a broader brand. Repeat buying, referrals and margin decide which one.

From November to February we run the product again, using the actual costs and feedback from October. These numbers should tell us which stage we reached. The people doing the work in Tamarin need to be able to run the next cycle without waiting for me. We will also review the plan with surfers in at least two communities outside Mauritius before deciding where to try it next.
